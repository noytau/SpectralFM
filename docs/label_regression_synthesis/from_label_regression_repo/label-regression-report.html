Where the label signal survives the backbone

A rebuild of the parameter_0 regression eval, probing every stage of the forward pass on the full labeled set (n=4,716 spectra) with a probe panel that includes PLS alongside OLS, Ridge and RidgeCV. Raw input, probed correctly, beats the backbone's own transformer output at every component count tested — 1, 2, 3, 7 and 12 — and the gap as a fraction of the ceiling is worst exactly where deployment is most likely to sit: 1 to 3 components.

03

Why raw input needs PLS, not RidgeCV

n=4,716 · 5-fold CV, PLS fitted inside each fold

Raw-input feature matrices are severely ill-conditioned — condition number ≈10⁹–10¹⁰ at every component count — because adjacent spectral samples are highly collinear. RidgeCV's isotropic shrinkage (XᵀX + αI) penalizes low-singular-value directions the hardest, and this raw-input signal lives exactly in the near-degenerate directions that collinearity creates. PLS doesn't shrink by singular value — it selects directions by covariance with the label — so it reaches the signal directly.

This is not a numerically fragile result. Three checks, all on the full n=4,716 set:

  • dtype-invariant: identical to 4 decimal places under float32 and float64, at every k tested (PLS's NIPALS fit, unlike a direct matrix solve, doesn't depend on precision here).
  • Stable across resampling: R²=0.8245±0.0008 (1-comp, k=64) and R²=0.9690±0.0002 (3-comp, k=128) across 8 independent fold-seed draws, all on n=4,716.
  • Shuffled-label canary: permuting the labels and re-running the identical fold-internal fit collapses every cell tested to ≈0 or below (e.g. 3-comp k=128: −0.080 on shuffled labels vs +0.969 on real ones) — the signal is real, not a supervised-projection leak.

Scope: the input(z), post-FE and post-projection columns in §02 are spot-checked with PLS at 1 and 3 components only (both show comparably large gains — e.g. z-scored input at 1-comp moves from 0.468 to 0.771) and have not been re-swept at every component count. Whether this also changes the few-shot numbers in §06 (n_train≤100) is untested.

05

Preprocessing and feature vector

steps 3 & 4 · n=4,716

Z-score at one component. Don't at two or three.

The cost of per-sample z-scoring flips sign. The mechanism is clean: z-scoring destroys cross-component scale relationships, which don't exist when there is only one component — there it merely improves conditioning.

At the embedding stage, plain concatenation is fine

PLS is a supervised projection: it must be fitted inside each cross-validation fold, not on the full dataset before cross-validating — fitting on everything lets the projection see test labels (measured leak: +0.083 to +0.090 R² at k=64, growing with k). 06

The 20-label question

step 5 · client constraint

The client expects ~20 labeled samples at most in the real setting. §01–§05 measure how much signal a probe recovers given all 4,716 labels — a question about the representation, not about sample efficiency. This section changes the protocol: repeated random subsampling with 100 draws per cell, drawing n_train labeled spectra from the 4,716-spectrum pool at a time, low-capacity probes, and distributions rather than point estimates (k-fold degenerates at n_train=20).

Conventional probes cannot do it. Ridge, PCA+ridge, PLS and k-NN all fail to beat predicting the mean at small n_train; the mean predictor is frequently the best of them. TabPFN changes that — it is the only probe that reliably clears the floor.

recommendation for the client conversation

At n_train=20, results are marginally viable — TabPFN on raw 2–3 component input, R²≈0.10–0.13, working ~90% of the time — and not viable with any conventional probe. But R²≈0.1 is a weak model. The curves show n_train=50–100 is where this becomes real (0.284 → 0.404 at 3-comp z-scored). The honest ask is to negotiate toward 50–100 labels, not to accept 20.

Not tested, and probably the biggest remaining win: which 20 spectra get labeled. These draws are random; stratified or active selection would likely beat them.

raw input wins in both regimes, by very different margins

At n=4,716 (all labels, §02–§03) raw input beats the embedding by 0.05–0.18 R², largest as a recovery-ratio gap at 1-comp. At n_train≤50 the same ranking holds but the margin is far larger — roughly 6× (3-comp, n_train=50, matched PCA-50: 0.458 raw vs 0.076 transformer). The embedding's disadvantage compounds at low sample sizes: it is both less label-informative in aggregate (§02–§03) and less label-efficient few-shot, which is exactly the distinction the client's constraint was probing.

07

Hypotheses this study falsified

including four of mine

These are kept rather than deleted: the failed hypotheses constrain the conclusions as much as the confirmed ones.

refuted
“More data is free R²”
False, and it depends on the probe. Over n_train = 500 → 4,716 at 2-comp, OLS degrades 0.735 → 0.665 while RidgeCV improves 0.706 → 0.756. They cross. Any single-n number is a point on a surface, not a constant.
refuted
“Cross-component ratios are the dominant carrier”
Too strong. Hand-engineered features — per-component moments plus pairwise differences and log-energy ratios — recover only 0.398 of raw input's 0.863, and a catastrophic 0.057 of the transformer's 0.793 (n=4,716). Scale information matters (z-scoring costs 0.05–0.07) but the signal is distributed across the full spectral shape.
refuted
“Components are never exchangeable” — over-generalised
The R²≈0.006 collapse is real for pooling raw signal per-component into one shared model — which reproduced the historical bf94422 bug from first principles and explained its mechanism. But averaging embeddings across components gives 0.661 at 3-comp (n=4,716): lossy, not collapse. The finding does not transfer.
refuted
“PCA-10 is why TabPFN failed on embeddings”
A reasonable hypothesis — the ceiling diagnostic showed PCA-10 retains only ~11% of the transformer's label signal versus 77% for raw input. Tested directly at k=50, where the transformer ceiling rises to 0.333: the gap did not close (n_train=50, §06). The embedding is genuinely less label-efficient, across two independent reductions.
revised
“T8's A/B was decided inside the noise”
An early assumption, and wrong. Split noise is 0.0005 (n=4,716) — resolvable ΔR² is ~0.001, so a 0.03 margin sits ~30× above it. Systematics (probe 0.27, sample size 0.07, normalisation 0.06–0.17) dominate by three orders of magnitude. The value of this work is a pinned protocol, not error bars.

What to do next

recommendations
  1. Update T6 and ARCHITECTURE.md. Replace “emb R²=0.44 beats input 0.38” with the §02/§03 numbers: raw input, probed with PLS, beats the embedding at every component count (n=4,716), worst as a recovery fraction at 1-comp (78%).
  2. Re-measure the reconstruction-trained backbones under this protocol. Their “≈0” scores come from the same under-powered protocol that misjudged this backbone's standing relative to raw input. They live on the RunAI PVC, not Geoffrey, so this was out of scope — but until it is done, the T6 ranking that the whole T8–T12 ladder was built on rests on measurements now known to be unreliable. Highest-value follow-up.
  3. Pin one protocol before comparing checkpoints. Probe choice alone moves R² by 0.27 (n=4,716, §02); comparing numbers measured under different unstated settings is the real hazard, not noise.
  4. Use PLS to probe raw input; use plain concatenation for the embedding. Add component-count-dependent z-scoring (z-score at 1-comp only). In the few-shot regime (n_train≤100), use raw input over the embedding regardless of probe.
  5. Re-sweep the input(z)/post-FE/post-projection columns with PLS at every component count, and check whether PLS changes the few-shot numbers in §06 — both flagged as open in §03.
  6. Fold the orphaned 2026-08-21 numbers into TASKS.md — they currently exist only in a YAML comment and a commit message.

Scope — what this does not cover

  • Only the Feb-25 SSL backbone. No reconstruction-trained checkpoints (PVC-only).
  • Pooling schemes (H4) and the trained attention-pooling head were not run — both need re-extraction at other poolings.
  • TabPFN excluded at 12-comp by compute budget: the full grid measured at ~29 GPU-hours.
  • Few-shot draws are random. Stratified or active selection is untested.
  • Distribution shift and calibration untested.
